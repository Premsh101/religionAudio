# Crime Corpus — Regional Coverage

| Region / tradition | Initial stories | Role in the corpus |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Poe: Rue Morgue, Marie Rogêt, Purloined Letter, Gold-Bug | Early detective, puzzle, coded-clue and crime fiction |
| United Kingdom | Sherlock Holmes: A Scandal in Bohemia, Red-Headed League, Boscombe Valley Mystery, Five Orange Pips, Speckled Band, Blue Carbuncle; Chesterton: The Blue Cross | Canonical Victorian/Edwardian detective and theft/murder mysteries |
| France | Arsène Lupin: The Arrest of Arsène Lupin (Morehead translation, PG 6133) | Gentleman-thief / heist tradition |
| India / Hindi | Gopal Ram Gahmari: Gerua Baba, Bhojpur ki Thagi | Early Hindi jasoosi and deception fiction |
| India / Urdu | Mirza Hadi Ruswa: Khooni Shahzada, Khooni Bhed | Early Urdu suspense and detective-related fiction |
| Arabic literary tradition | The Three Apples, The Hunchback | Early murder mystery, mistaken accusation and chain-of-confession structures |

## Four-language publication plan

Each story should ultimately have:

1. original/source language version;
2. English;
3. Hindi;
4. Arabic;
5. Urdu.

For source-language stories, the source version counts as one of the four. For all other languages, produce a faithful translation from the canonical source, not a translation-of-a-translation.

## Discovery notes

Urdu literary scholarship identifies very early detective-fiction activity, including Khan Ahmed Hussain's *Shaheen Chor* (1897), early Sherlock Holmes translations/adaptations, and later Mirza Hadi Ruswa crime titles. Gopal Ram Gahmari is likewise documented as a major early Hindi detective writer with works including *Gerua Baba* and *Bhojpur ki Thagi*. These are cataloged for provenance; the current repository does not treat third-party scans as automatically cleared for commercial redistribution.

# Rituals & Customs Stories

This collection contains **actual ritual and custom story text** in English, Hindi, Arabic and Urdu.

## Editorial standard

Every record is a repository-authored **ORIGINAL_FAITHFUL_RETELLING**. The wording is newly written for the corpus and does not reproduce a modern copyrighted translation.

Each record explains the traditional origin story behind a ritual or custom that is still practised, and keeps the layers apart:

- **Scriptural narratives** — origin stories found in sacred texts (Torah, Gospels, Qur'an).
- **Hadith, rabbinic and commentarial traditions** — later authoritative traditions (hadith collections, Talmud, Pali commentaries, Jain texts).
- **Popular legends** — devotional retellings communities use to explain a custom, even where they are not found in the classical text (for example, the Draupadi-Krishna rakhi story or Karna and Pitru Paksha).
- **Living practice** — how the ritual is performed today, described as documented practice rather than as proof of the story behind it.

Religious sensitivity rules applied to every record:
- God is never physically depicted.
- The Prophet Muhammad is never physically described and is given no invented speech.
- Miracles are presented as belief or tradition, not as scientific fact.
- Regional and denominational variation is acknowledged rather than collapsed into one "correct" version.

## Rights and source policy

The repository stores original retellings rather than modern copyrighted translations. Source references (see `sourceBasis` on each record and `data/sources/registry.json`) are retained for scriptural, historical and cultural verification.

Before commercial publication, human review is required for:
- theology and religious terminology for each tradition
- names, honorifics and transliteration
- regional and denominational differences
- Arabic and Urdu language quality
- pronunciation and TTS suitability
- rights metadata for any future direct-source ingestion
